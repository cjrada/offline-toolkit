# Australia: first aid and emergency notes (kit summary - verify against current ANZCOR / St John / Red Cross guidance)

NOT MEDICAL ADVICE. This is a short memory aid written for this kit, not professional advice or training,
and it may be out of date. Always check current guidance (ANZCOR, St John Ambulance, Australian Red Cross,
Healthdirect) and call 000 in an emergency. Use at your own risk.

These notes override older US military advice in the other manuals where they conflict. They summarise
widely published Australian first aid practice (ANZCOR guidelines). They are a memory aid, not training.
Do a real first aid course (St John, Red Cross, Surf Life Saving) and replace this file when guidelines change.

## Emergency numbers (Australia)
Emergency (police, fire, ambulance): 000. From a mobile with no coverage on your network: 112 also works.
Text/TTY: 106. SES (flood, storm help): 132 500. Poisons Information Centre: 13 11 26 (24 hours).
Healthdirect nurse advice: 1800 022 222. Queensland: 13 HEALTH (13 43 25 84).

## Snake bite (all Australian snakes) - pressure immobilisation bandage
Australian practice for snake bite is the pressure immobilisation technique, NOT a tourniquet or a
constricting band, NOT cutting or sucking the bite, and NOT washing the bite (venom on the skin can identify the snake).
Do not try to catch or kill the snake. Call 000.
1. Keep the person still and calm; lie them down. Movement spreads venom through the lymph system.
2. Apply a broad elastic (or crepe) bandage over the bite site firmly, then bandage the whole limb from the
   fingers or toes upward as far as possible, as firm as for a sprain. Mark the bite site on the bandage.
3. Splint the limb to stop movement (a stick, rolled newspaper, or bandage the legs together).
4. Do not remove the bandage; leave that to hospital. Watch breathing; start CPR if needed.
Most bites are on a limb. For bites on the body or face, apply firm pressure over the site if possible and keep still.

## Funnel-web spider and mouse spider bite
Treat exactly like a snake bite: pressure immobilisation bandage, splint, keep still, call 000.

## Redback spider and most other spider bites
Do NOT use a pressure bandage for redback bites (it can increase pain). Wash, apply an ice pack or cold
compress to relieve pain, and seek medical advice if pain is severe, spreads, or there is sweating or vomiting.

## Box jellyfish and Irukandji (tropical waters, northern Australia)
Call 000. Get the person out of the water. Pour vinegar liberally over the stung area for at least 30 seconds
to deactivate stingers that have not fired yet, then remove tentacles. Start CPR if needed.
Do not rub the area and do not use fresh water.

## Bluebottle (non-tropical waters)
Remove tentacles, rinse with sea water, then put the area in hot water (as hot as comfortable) for 20 minutes.
If no hot water, use a cold pack. Do not use vinegar for bluebottles.

## Blue-ringed octopus and cone shell
Pressure immobilisation bandage if on a limb, call 000, and be ready to give rescue breathing / CPR for a long
time: these venoms paralyse breathing while the person may still be aware.

## Heat illness (common in Queensland summers)
Heat stroke (hot, confused, may stop sweating) is an emergency: call 000, move to shade, remove extra clothing,
cool aggressively with water and fanning, ice packs to neck, armpits and groin. Give sips of water only if fully alert.

## Bushfire
Follow the local fire service and the Australian Warning System (Advice, Watch and Act, Emergency Warning).
Leaving early is the safest option. If trapped, shelter in a solid building or a cleared area away from fuel,
cover skin with natural-fibre clothing, and drink water.

## Floods
Never drive, walk or swim through floodwater ("If it's flooded, forget it"). Floodwater can carry sewage and
debris; treat any water you drink by boiling or disinfection.

## Water
Boil water at a rolling boil for at least 1 minute to make it safe from germs. If you cannot boil, use
water purification tablets as directed, or plain unscented household bleach as directed by health authorities.
Boiling does not remove chemicals or fuel contamination.

## Plants, mushrooms, medicines
Do not eat wild mushrooms or unknown plants based on an AI answer or a photo app - deaths from death cap
mushrooms occur in Australia. Do not take medication doses from the AI; use the packet, a pharmacist,
Healthdirect, or a doctor.
